Support for families facing childhood cancer.

Nala’s Story

In July 2016, our daughter Nala was diagnosed with leukemia. She was three years old and has Down syndrome. Doctors and nurses cared for her through treatment, and wonderful foundations helped our family get through those difficult days. It was the toughest two and a half years of her life!

A Message From Nala’s Family During Treatment

When you wear your #NalaStrong shirt, know that you are wearing more than just a shirt. On your shoulders you are representing a three-year-old fighter who has faced more pain than any three-year-old should have to face. You represent a strong little girl who has endured countless needles and pokes, chemotherapy, tears, surgeries, tests, transfusions, long nights, early mornings, medications, spinal taps, echocardiograms, and mouth sores.

Thank you to all of you who have been a part of Nala’s journey. So many kids shouldn’t have to face the reality of childhood cancer, but they are, and they are kicking cancer’s butt!

Say a prayer for every child fighting this life-threatening disease. If you are looking for a good cause to donate to, please consider a foundation that researches cures for childhood cancer.

Looking Back Ten Years Later

The latest social media trend was the ten-year challenge, and I wanted to see if there was something I could share. It’s always meaningful to look back and see how far you’ve come. But as I scrolled through old photos, I realized something I tried so hard to forget. 2016, without question, was the hardest year of my life. That July, we learned that our sweet, precious Nala had cancer.

The months leading up to her diagnosis were already filled with so many emotions. She was supposed to start public school, and I was terrified. She was only three, and I didn’t feel she was ready for such a big school. Up until then, she had always been in a small, private daycare setting, and that felt safe. That felt like enough. I remember sitting there trying to figure out logistics. How will I get her to school? How will I pick her up? Public school is so different. You can’t drop them off early or keep them late, and I was trying to make it all work while reassuring myself that this was simply the next step in her journey.

At the same time, I had just stepped into a new opportunity at work, one I absolutely loved, with a company I truly adored. I felt fulfilled, excited, and hopeful about the future. And then, within a matter of weeks, everything changed.

We get so caught up in our routines, convinced that tomorrow will always be there and that we can handle things later. What I learned in 2016 is that nothing is guaranteed. Life really can change in an instant, and that’s exactly what happened to my family.

2016 was a hard year for me. As I look at this challenge now, I also see how far we’ve come. I want to share a few photos from the year that forever transformed me. As we approach ten years, my hope is that this serves as a reminder of just how fragile and precious life truly is: our health, our relationships, and the love and closeness we share.

Please, live every day like it matters, because it does. Love your people. Hug them tighter. Tell them you love them. Take care of your body. If you ever have the chance to lend a helping hand, do it. Don’t expect anything in return. Just do it because you can.

2026 brings a huge accomplishment for Nala, and I pray for many years ahead of health, happiness, and all the good things for her. We still face many side effects from the treatment, but to have her here, still smiling and being the best big sister and daughter in the world, means everything to us. Here’s to a transformative ten years and many more to come. #NalaStrong forever.

Why We’re Starting NalaStrong

We’re starting NalaStrong to offer other families the same kind of care we received. When a child is newly diagnosed with cancer, we want their family to know there are people they can turn to for practical help and support.
